Research

Making batteries safer through mechanics and thermal science.

My research connects material degradation, cell-level failure, and pack-level hazards. The goal is to turn physical mechanisms into measurable safety indicators and predictive tools.

01

Aging & thermal runaway

Quantifying how lithium plating, SEI growth, usable energy, and operating history reshape thermal runaway onset and severity.

02

Internal short circuits

Resolving separator and electrode-stack failure under mechanical loading, from soft short circuits to hard shorts and thermal consequences.

03

Physics-informed inference

Combining inverse modeling, chemical kinetics, and machine learning to identify hidden degradation states from thermal and electrochemical data.

04

Cross-scale battery safety

Linking material-level reactions with cell temperature fields and propagation behavior to support safer battery design and testing.

About

From fundamental failure mechanisms to practical safety metrics.

I am a Ph.D. student in Mechanical Engineering at The Pennsylvania State University, advised by Prof. Jun Xu. My work uses controlled abuse experiments, thermal diagnostics, electrochemical characterization, and multiphysics modeling to investigate battery safety.

I am especially interested in how aging pathways create hidden safety liabilities, and how those changes can be inferred before a hazardous event occurs.
